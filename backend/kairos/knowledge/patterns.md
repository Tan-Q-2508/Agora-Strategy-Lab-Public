# Chart patterns

## How to trade any chart pattern
tags: pattern breakout confirmation neckline target measured move stop invalidation volume
A pattern is only a hypothesis until price confirms it with a close beyond the trigger line (Kairos requires 0.1 × ATR beyond). Before that it is "forming" and fails often. The measured-move target projects the pattern's height from the breakout. The stop goes where the idea is proved wrong (beyond the opposite side or the last swing). Volume at the breakout of 1.3× average or more improves the odds. Failed patterns are signals too: a failed bull flag frequently leads to a fast drop because trapped buyers sell. Always check the pattern's measured history in this market rather than textbook success rates.

## Double top and double bottom
tags: double top double bottom m pattern w pattern reversal neckline double_top double_bottom
A double top is two peaks at about the same price with a dip between; it confirms when price closes below the dip's low (the neckline), and the target is the neckline minus the pattern's height. A double bottom is the mirror (a "W"). They are reversal patterns, so they need a prior trend to reverse. Second peaks on lighter volume are the textbook version.

## Triple top and triple bottom
tags: triple top triple bottom reversal resistance support triple_top triple_bottom
Three tests of the same level that hold. When the dips between the peaks are also at the same level, it is really a rectangle (a range) and can break either way.

## Head and shoulders, inverse head and shoulders
tags: head and shoulders inverse head shoulders reversal neckline head_shoulders inverse_head_shoulders
A head and shoulders top has a higher middle peak (the head) between two lower, similar peaks (the shoulders); the neckline joins the two dips. A close below the neckline confirms it; the target is the head's height below the neckline, projected down from the breakout. The inverse version marks bottoms. Symmetry of the shoulders in price and time makes the pattern more reliable; a neckline that slopes sharply makes it less so.

## Triangles: ascending, descending, symmetrical
tags: triangle ascending descending symmetrical coil converging breakout apex ascending_triangle descending_triangle symmetrical_triangle
Triangles are pauses where the range narrows. Ascending triangles (flat top, rising lows) usually break up; descending triangles (falling highs, flat bottom) usually break down; symmetrical triangles break either way, more often in the direction of the trend before them. Breakouts work best in the first two-thirds of the way to the apex; a pattern that drifts all the way to its apex loses its force. Target: the triangle's widest height added to the breakout.

## Wedges: rising and falling
tags: rising wedge falling wedge reversal converging rising_wedge falling_wedge
A rising wedge (both lines rising, converging) shows buying losing force and usually breaks down; a falling wedge usually breaks up. They often appear at the end of trends.

## Flags and pennants
tags: flag pennant bull flag bear flag continuation pole consolidation bull_flag bear_flag bull_pennant bear_pennant
After a sharp move (the pole) the price rests in a small channel sloping against the move (a flag) or a small triangle (a pennant), usually for 5–20 candles and retracing less than half the pole. A breakout in the pole's direction continues the trend; the target is the pole's length added to the breakout. A bear flag is the upside-down version: a sharp drop, a gentle upward drift, then another leg down. These are among the more reliable continuation patterns when the pole is steep and the volume dries up during the flag.

## Rectangles and ranges
tags: rectangle range trading range support resistance box rectangle
Price bouncing between a flat ceiling and a flat floor. Inside the range, traders buy near support and sell near resistance; a close outside the range with volume starts a move about as large as the range's height.

## Cup and handle
tags: cup and handle rounding bottom continuation cup_handle
A rounded, U-shaped bottom between two similar highs (the rims), followed by a small, shallow dip (the handle) in the upper half of the cup. The breakout above the rim targets the cup's depth added to the rim. Deep (over 50%) or V-shaped cups are less reliable.

## Broadening formation
tags: broadening megaphone expanding volatility broadening
Swings getting wider in both directions: higher highs and lower lows. It signals an emotional, unstable market. It is the least reliable pattern and a reason to trade smaller.

## Support and resistance
tags: support resistance zone level flip role reversal round number
Support is a price area where buying has repeatedly stopped declines; resistance is where selling has stopped rallies. They are zones, not exact lines. A level matters more when it was touched several times, recently, with heavy volume, or when it flipped (old resistance becoming support after a breakout). The more times a level is tested, the weaker it can become: each test absorbs orders waiting there.

## Trendlines and channels
tags: trendline channel rising falling slope break
A rising support line joins higher lows; a falling resistance line joins lower highs. Two touches draw a line, three confirm it. A decisive close through a long-standing trendline is an early warning that the trend is changing, though not proof. Parallel support and resistance lines form a channel; traders buy near the lower line of a rising channel.

## Divergences
tags: divergence regular hidden bullish bearish rsi macd momentum
Regular bearish divergence: price makes a higher high while RSI makes a lower high, so buying power is fading. Regular bullish divergence is the mirror at lows. Hidden divergences suggest the trend is resting rather than ending (for example a higher low in price with a lower low in RSI during an uptrend). Divergences can persist for a while; they are warnings that need a price trigger.

## Fibonacci retracements
tags: fibonacci retracement 38.2 50 61.8 extension golden ratio pullback
After a big move, pullbacks often pause near 38.2%, 50% or 61.8% of it. A pullback that holds the 38.2–61.8% band and turns back up is a common trend-continuation entry. Extensions (127.2%, 161.8%) are common targets once the old high breaks. Fibonacci levels work best where they line up with other support or resistance.
