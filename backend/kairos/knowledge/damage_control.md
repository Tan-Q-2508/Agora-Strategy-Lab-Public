# Damage control: when to go in and when to pull out

## Kairos's news guard (damage control)
tags: news guard damage control pull out go in protective cautious kairos desk news risk bad news negative headlines what does the desk do sell exit
Kairos's desk reads a news risk gauge (0–100) for its market and each stock's own headlines, and follows fixed rules (setting "News guard"). Protective mode (the default): when risk is watchful (25–50) new trades open at half size; elevated (50–75): no new long trades, and long trades that are losing are closed; severe (75+): no new trades at all, and long trades are closed once prices confirm (the watchlist fell 1% or more on the candle) or when the gauge reads 90+. Red-flag company news (fraud, raids, defaults, profit warnings, the CEO quitting) closes a long in that stock; negative company news blocks new longs; a positive catalyst adds 10 points to the score. Cautious mode only stops new trades. Shorts are left to their stops. Going back in is automatic: news ages (half-life 6 hours), the gauge cools, and the brain trades its signals again. The guard never invents orders: it only stops, shrinks or closes what the brain's code does, and every action is written in the desk diary.

## The news risk gauge
tags: news risk gauge calm watchful elevated severe level score corroboration
The gauge reads the last 36 hours of world and market news: each story's severity (0–5), how risk-off it reads, how recent it is (half-life 6 hours), whether it touches markets at all, and how many different publishers report it (one blog post is weak, five agencies are strong). The heaviest story counts fully and each next one less, so an ongoing war's daily coverage doesn't by itself mean danger. Calming news (a ceasefire, a rate cut, a deal) lowers it. Calm < 25, watchful 25–50, elevated 50–75, severe 75+.

## A professional's damage-control playbook
tags: risk off playbook reduce exposure raise cash tighten stops hedge drawdown control
Professionals respond to shocks by cutting risk, not by predicting: (1) stop opening new positions; (2) cut losing trades first, they are the ones telling you you are wrong; (3) tighten stops on winners rather than selling them all; (4) reduce size so the money at risk stays the same when volatility doubles; (5) avoid selling into the first panicky minutes after a gap down unless a stop is hit, because opening prices after shocks are often the worst of the day; (6) write down what would make you buy again. Hedging with index futures or options is the institutional tool; Agora has no derivatives, so cutting exposure is the hedge.

## When to go back in
tags: re entry going back in after crash buy the dip recovery capitulation
Signs a shock is passing: the news risk gauge falls back below 50, the index reclaims its 20-day average, new lows stop expanding, volatility (VIX or India VIX) falls from its peak, and bad news stops pushing prices lower. Historically most geopolitical shocks bottomed within three to four weeks and recovered within two to three months (LPL Research); the exceptions came with recessions. Re-enter in steps (a third at a time) rather than all at once, and only on your strategy's signals, not on hope.

## Event risk: when not to hold a trade
tags: event risk earnings results budget rbi policy fomc expiry gap risk overnight
Scheduled events cause gaps that stops can't protect against: company results, the Union Budget (1 February), RBI policy days, US Fed decisions, CPI and jobs reports, election results, and index expiry days. Professional swing traders reduce or close positions before events they can't analyse, or size them so a 10% gap is survivable. A stop-loss is an order at the next available price: after a gap it fills at the gap, not at the stop.

## Gap risk and circuit limits
tags: gap risk circuit limit stock band lower circuit frozen illiquid
In India each stock has a daily price band (2%, 5%, 10% or 20%; F&O stocks have wider, dynamic limits). A stock locked at its lower circuit can't be sold that day: sellers queue with no buyers. That is the real meaning of "can't get out". Diversifying, keeping position sizes small and avoiding illiquid small caps are the defences. Agora's fill model detects locked circuits and refuses fills there.

## Never: panic, revenge, doubling down
tags: panic selling revenge trading doubling down averaging down fear capitulation psychology
The three costliest reactions to a shock: panic-selling everything at the low after the move has happened, revenge trading to win back losses, and doubling down on a losing position because it is "cheaper". Rules decided in calm times (stops, maximum risk, the news guard) exist to stop exactly these.
