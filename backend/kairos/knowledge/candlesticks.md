# Candlestick patterns

## Reading candlesticks
tags: candlestick candle body wick shadow japanese
Each candle shows the open, high, low and close. A long body shows conviction; long wicks show prices that were rejected. One- to three-candle patterns are short-term signals: they matter at support or resistance and in the right context (a hammer after a fall), and much less in the middle of nowhere. Their edge on their own is small; use them for timing, together with trend, levels and volume.

## Hammer, hanging man, inverted hammer, shooting star
tags: hammer hanging man inverted hammer shooting star wick rejection reversal
A hammer has a small body near the top and a long lower wick after a decline: sellers pushed down, buyers pushed back. The same shape after a rise is a hanging man, a warning. An inverted hammer (long upper wick after a decline) shows buyers testing higher; after a rise the shape is a shooting star, where buyers were pushed back down.

## Engulfing, harami, piercing line, dark cloud cover
tags: engulfing harami piercing dark cloud reversal two candle
A bullish engulfing candle's body covers the whole previous down candle after a decline; a bearish engulfing is the mirror after a rise. A harami is a small candle inside the previous large one, showing the move stalling. A piercing line opens below the previous close and closes above the middle of the previous down candle; dark cloud cover is its bearish mirror.

## Morning star, evening star, three soldiers, three crows
tags: morning star evening star three white soldiers three black crows reversal three candle
A morning star is a large down candle, a small pause candle, then a strong up candle closing past the first one's middle: a bottoming signal. The evening star is the top version. Three white soldiers are three strong up candles each opening inside the previous body; three black crows are the bearish version. They are more reliable than one-candle patterns.

## Doji family, spinning top, marubozu, inside and outside bars
tags: doji dragonfly gravestone long legged spinning top marubozu inside bar outside bar indecision
A doji (open ≈ close) shows balance; a dragonfly doji (long lower wick) after a fall is bullish, a gravestone doji (long upper wick) after a rise bearish. Spinning tops show indecision. A marubozu (body with almost no wicks) shows one side in full control. An inside bar (inside the previous range) shows coiling before a move; an outside bar takes out both the previous high and low.

## Tweezer tops and bottoms
tags: tweezer tweezer_top tweezer_bottom matching highs lows double test
Two candles with almost the same high after a rise (tweezer top) or the same low after a fall (tweezer bottom): a price tested twice and rejected. Most meaningful when the second candle closes the other way (a bearish candle at a tweezer top) and at a known support or resistance level; on their own they are weak signals.
