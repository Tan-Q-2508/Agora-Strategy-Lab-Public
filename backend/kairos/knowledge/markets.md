# Markets: India and the US

## Indian market structure (NSE)
tags: india nse nifty sensex market hours 9:15 15:30 circuit t+1 delivery intraday
NSE trades 09:15–15:30 IST, Monday to Friday, with a pre-open session before. The Nifty 50 holds 50 large companies, with banks, IT, energy and consumer stocks the heaviest. Settlement is T+1. Individual stocks have price bands (circuit limits); a stock locked at its band can't be traded that day. Delivery trades are long-only in Agora; shorting is allowed only intraday, and intraday positions are squared off by 15:20 IST.

## What moves Indian stocks
tags: india fii dii flows rbi rupee crude oil monsoon budget results gst
Foreign institutional investor (FII) flows, domestic institutional (DII) flows, RBI interest-rate decisions, the rupee, crude oil prices (India imports most of its oil), the Union Budget (early February), quarterly results seasons (mid-January, April, July, October), monthly F&O expiry, global risk mood (US markets overnight, Asian markets in the morning) and sector news.

## US market structure
tags: us nyse nasdaq market hours 9:30 16:00 s&p 500 spy qqq
US regular hours are 09:30–16:00 New York time (evening in India). Agora ignores pre- and post-market trading. The S&P 500 (SPY) is the main benchmark; QQQ tracks the Nasdaq-100, heavy in large technology companies. Shorting is allowed; borrow fees aren't modelled.

## What moves US stocks
tags: us fed fomc cpi inflation jobs earnings season rates treasury yields dollar ai
Federal Reserve decisions (eight FOMC meetings a year), inflation (CPI) and jobs reports, Treasury yields, earnings seasons (mid-January, April, July, October), guidance from the largest technology companies, and themes such as AI spending. Monthly options expiry (third Friday) can move prices around large strike levels.

## Seasonality and calendar effects
tags: seasonality calendar earnings season holiday turn of month
Calendar effects (turn-of-month strength, pre-holiday drift, "sell in May") are small and unreliable after costs. Event risk is real: avoid opening new positions just before a company's results unless the strategy is designed for it.

## Reading the news like a trader
tags: news sentiment headlines priced in reaction
What matters is the surprise, not the news: a "great" result that was already expected can make a stock fall. Watch the price's reaction: good news that can't lift the price is bearish; bad news that can't push it down is bullish. Headlines are noisy; one article rarely changes a trend. Kairos uses news sentiment as a filter, not a trigger.
