# Strategies traders use around the world

## Turtle trading
tags: turtle turtles richard dennis donchian breakout trend following system 2 position sizing n
In 1983 Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt taught novices ("Turtles") a complete mechanical system: buy a break of the 20-day (System 1) or 55-day (System 2) high, size every position so that one ATR ("N") costs 1% of the account, stop 2N away, add units every half N, and exit on a 10- or 20-day low. Many small losses, a few enormous trends. Agora's "Turtle trading" template is System 2 without pyramiding. It needs many uncorrelated markets to shine; on single stocks it is choppier.

## Golden cross and moving-average trend systems
tags: golden_cross ma_crossover triple_ma 4 9 18 moving average crossover system trend
The simplest trend systems trade moving-average crossings: 50/200 (golden cross), 20/50, or three averages in order (4-9-18 EMAs on futures desks). They differ only in speed. All lose in sideways markets and profit in long trends; combining one with an ADX or choppiness filter skips some chop.

## Connors RSI(2) and short-term mean reversion
tags: connors_rsi2 connors rsi 2 mean reversion pullback larry connors cesar alvarez
Larry Connors and Cesar Alvarez's research (Short Term Trading Strategies That Work, 2008): in stocks and indices above their 200-day average, buying when the 2-day RSI falls under 10 (or after several lower closes) and selling on a close above the 5-day average has historically had high win rates and short holding periods. Weaknesses: slippage and costs eat the small average gain, and an uptrend filter is essential.

## Elder's triple screen
tags: triple_screen elder triple screen alexander elder multiple timeframe
Alexander Elder (Trading for a Living, 1993): screen one picks the direction from a longer timeframe (weekly trend), screen two waits for a pullback on the trading timeframe (an oscillator such as stochastic or force index going oversold), screen three times the entry (a break of the previous high). Agora's template uses a 130-day EMA for the weekly trend and the stochastic for the pullback.

## Squeeze breakout (TTM)
tags: squeeze_breakout ttm squeeze john carter volatility breakout
John Carter (Mastering the Trade): trade the release of a volatility squeeze (Bollinger Bands inside the Keltner Channel) in the direction of momentum, exiting when momentum fades. It catches explosive moves but also many false starts.

## Ichimoku trading
tags: ichimoku_cloud ichimoku kinko hyo hosoda tenkan kijun cloud chikou
Goichi Hosoda's Ichimoku Kinko Hyo ("one-glance equilibrium chart", published 1969): price above the cloud is bullish, the conversion line (9) crossing the base line (26) gives entries, the cloud is support, and the lagging line confirms. Strong in clean trends, confusing in ranges. Agora's template buys a conversion/base cross with the price above both cloud spans and exits below the base line.

## Parabolic SAR and Keltner breakout
tags: psar_trend keltner_breakout parabolic sar stop and reverse keltner channel raschke
Welles Wilder's Parabolic SAR (1978) is a stop-and-reverse system: always in, flipping when the price touches the dots. In trends it rides well; in ranges it whipsaws badly. Keltner breakouts (Chester Keltner 1960, ATR form by Linda Raschke) buy a close beyond the ATR channel and exit at the middle line.

## ADX trend and Aroon
tags: adx_trend aroon_trend vortex_cross wilder directional movement aroon chande vortex
Welles Wilder's directional movement system: buy when +DI crosses above −DI with ADX above 20–25, exit on the cross back. Tushar Chande's Aroon and the vortex indicator give similar trend-start signals. All are trend filters first and entry signals second.

## Oscillator pullbacks: Williams %R, CCI, MFI, Fisher, Schaff
tags: williams_r_pullback cci_swing mfi_reversion fisher_reversal schaff_cycle oscillator pullback swing trading
Swing traders buy when an oscillator leaves its oversold zone in a stock that is still in a long uptrend: Williams %R back above −80, CCI back above −100, MFI back above 20, the Fisher transform turning up from a deep low, the Schaff cycle crossing up through 25. They sell when the oscillator reaches the opposite extreme or after a few days. Win rates are high, average gains small; stops and costs matter.

## Heikin-Ashi and Coppock trend trades
tags: heikin_ashi_trend coppock_turn heikin ashi coppock long term
Heikin-Ashi trend trading stays in while the averaged candles keep one colour, filtering single-candle noise. The Coppock turn buys when the Coppock curve crosses zero: a slow, long-horizon timing tool originally for monthly index data.

## VWAP and opening-range intraday trading
tags: vwap_pullback opening_range_breakout vwap orb intraday day trading
Intraday traders treat VWAP as the day's fair price: above it buyers are winning, and institutions benchmark their fills to it. A reclaim of VWAP after the first half hour is a common long entry; a loss of it an exit. Opening-range breakouts trade a break of the first 15–30 minutes' range (Toby Crabel). In India every intraday position must be closed by 15:20; in Agora intraday templates always exit before the close.

## CANSLIM (William O'Neil)
tags: canslim o'neil investors business daily cup with handle growth stocks
William O'Neil's CANSLIM: Current quarterly earnings up strongly, Annual earnings growth, New products or highs, Supply and demand (volume), Leader not laggard (high relative strength), Institutional sponsorship, Market direction (only buy in confirmed uptrends). Entries are breakouts from bases such as the cup with handle; losses are cut at 7–8%. Agora covers the technical half (bases, breakouts, relative strength, market trend); earnings data isn't in Agora.

## Minervini's SEPA and the volatility contraction pattern (VCP)
tags: minervini sepa vcp volatility contraction pattern stage 2 trend template
Mark Minervini (Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard): only buy stocks in a "stage 2" uptrend (price above a rising 150- and 200-day average, the 50-day above both, within 25% of the 52-week high), forming a VCP: a base whose pullbacks get smaller (for example 25%, 12%, 6%) with drying volume, then a breakout on volume. Stops are tight (often under 8%).

## Weinstein's stage analysis
tags: weinstein stage analysis stage 1 2 3 4 30 week moving average secular
Stan Weinstein (Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets, 1988): every stock cycles through stage 1 (base, flat 30-week average), stage 2 (advance: price above a rising 30-week average, the only stage to buy), stage 3 (top, average flattening) and stage 4 (decline, never own). On daily charts the 30-week average is about the 150-day.

## Darvas boxes
tags: darvas box nicolas darvas breakout new highs
Nicolas Darvas (How I Made $2,000,000 in the Stock Market, 1960) bought stocks making new highs when they broke out of a "box" (a tight range), with a stop at the bottom of the box, trailing it up box by box. A precursor of modern momentum breakout trading.

## Jesse Livermore's pivotal points
tags: livermore pivotal points line of least resistance reminiscences
Jesse Livermore (Reminiscences of a Stock Operator; How to Trade in Stocks, 1940) waited for the "line of least resistance": he bought only when the price broke through a pivotal point (a prior high), added only to winners, and cut losses fast. "It never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It always was my sitting."

## Momentum investing (cross-sectional and time-series)
tags: momentum cross sectional time series jegadeesh titman dual momentum antonacci trend following
Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) showed that buying the past 3–12 months' winners and avoiding losers outperformed for decades worldwide, including in India; it can crash sharply when markets rebound from bear markets (2009). Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen 2012) buys what is above its own past price. Gary Antonacci's dual momentum combines both: hold the strongest asset only if it beats cash.

## Factor investing: value, quality, low volatility, size
tags: factor value quality low volatility size fama french smart beta
Academic factors with long records: value (cheap on earnings or book), quality (profitable, low debt), low volatility (calmer stocks have historically given similar returns with less risk), size (small caps, weaker evidence lately) and momentum. They need fundamental data and long holding periods; Agora focuses on price-based strategies, but a low-volatility or momentum tilt can be built from prices alone.

## Pairs trading and statistical arbitrage
tags: pairs trading statistical arbitrage cointegration market neutral spread
Trade two related stocks (two private banks, Coca-Cola and Pepsi): when their price ratio stretches far from normal (a z-score beyond ±2), short the rich one and buy the cheap one, closing when it reverts. Market-neutral in theory; in practice relationships break. Agora backtests one stock at a time, so pairs aren't modelled.

## Earnings and event strategies
tags: earnings post earnings announcement drift pead gap and go event driven
Post-earnings-announcement drift (Ball and Brown 1968; Bernard and Thomas 1989): stocks keep drifting in the direction of an earnings surprise for weeks. Gap-and-go traders buy strong gaps on news that hold above the first candle's low. Event strategies need an earnings calendar; Agora reads news but has no earnings calendar yet.

## Swing, position and scalping styles
tags: swing trading position trading scalping day trading holding period style
Scalpers hold seconds to minutes and live on costs and spreads (impractical for retail in India after STT). Day traders close everything by the end of the day. Swing traders hold days to weeks, trading pullbacks and breakouts. Position traders hold weeks to months on trends. Longer holding periods cut costs and noise; shorter ones need more precision and discipline.

## Buy and hold, SIPs and asset allocation
tags: buy and hold sip systematic investment plan rupee cost averaging asset allocation 60 40 index fund
The benchmark every strategy must beat: holding a low-cost index fund. A SIP (systematic investment plan) buys a fixed amount every month, buying more units when prices are low. Over 15+ years Indian and US large-cap indices have rewarded patient holders despite crashes. Many active strategies' real value is lower drawdowns, not higher returns.

## Strategies to avoid: martingale, averaging down, grid bots
tags: martingale averaging down grid trading doubling down ruin
Doubling the stake after each loss (martingale), adding to losers without a plan, or grid bots that buy every dip in a falling stock work until they don't: a long trend wipes out years of small gains. They have positive-looking win rates and catastrophic tails. Kairos never sizes up after losses.

## Position sizing: fixed fractional and the Kelly criterion
tags: kelly criterion position sizing fixed fractional optimal f risk of ruin
Fixed-fractional sizing risks the same small share of equity on every trade (Kairos: 1%). The Kelly criterion (f = edge ÷ odds) gives the growth-maximising bet but is far too aggressive for uncertain edges; professionals use a quarter to a half of Kelly at most. When the edge is estimated from a backtest, assume it is smaller than it looks.
