# The full candlestick dictionary

## How reliable candlestick patterns are
tags: candlestick reliability bulkowski statistics performance best candles
Thomas Bulkowski tested candlestick patterns on thousands of stocks. His best performers (by overall rank): the bearish three-line strike (which acted as a *bullish* reversal 84% of the time), the bullish three-line strike (a bearish reversal 65%), three black crows (bearish reversal 78%), the evening star (72%), the upside tasuki gap (bullish continuation 57%), the inverted hammer (a bearish continuation 65%), matching low (bearish continuation 61%), the bullish abandoned baby (70%), two black gapping candles (bearish continuation 68%) and the bearish breakaway (63%). Two lessons: many patterns do the opposite of their textbook name, and most candles barely beat a coin flip on their own. Kairos therefore measures every candle on your own market's history and weights it by what actually happened.

## Abandoned baby
tags: abandoned_baby_bull abandoned_baby_bear abandoned baby island doji gap reversal
A large candle, a doji left alone above (or below) a gap, then a gap the other way into a strong candle closing well into the first one. It is a miniature island reversal and one of the rarest and most reliable three-candle patterns (Bulkowski: the bullish version reversed 70% of the time).

## Doji stars, morning and evening doji stars, tri-star
tags: doji_star_bull doji_star_bear morning_doji_star evening_doji_star tristar_bull tristar_bear doji star
A doji star is a doji that gaps away from a long candle: the trend hesitates. Confirmed by a strong candle the other way it becomes a morning doji star (at a bottom) or an evening doji star (at a top), stronger than ordinary stars because the middle candle is pure indecision. A tri-star (three dojis with the middle one gapping) is extremely rare; it marks exhaustion after a long trend.

## Three inside and three outside
tags: three_inside_up three_inside_down three_outside_up three_outside_down harami engulfing confirmation
Three inside up is a bullish harami (a small candle inside a big down candle) confirmed by a third candle closing above the first candle's open; three inside down is the mirror. Three outside up is a bullish engulfing confirmed by a third up candle closing higher; three outside down is its mirror. The third candle is the confirmation that two-candle patterns lack, which is why these do better than plain haramis and engulfings.

## Three-line strike
tags: three_line_strike_bull three_line_strike_bear three line strike four candle
Three candles in one direction, then one long candle the other way that wipes all three out. Traditional books call the version after three up candles bullish (a pause that resets); Bulkowski's data found the opposite: after three down candles and a big up "strike" the price kept rising 84% of the time, the best candle result he recorded. Treat it as the strike candle's direction winning.

## Two crows, upside gap two crows, two black gapping
tags: two_crows upside_gap_two_crows two_black_gapping crows gap bearish
After a strong up candle the price gaps up but closes down, then a second down candle drives back into (two crows) or engulfs the first down candle while holding above the gap (upside gap two crows): the gap is failing. Two black gapping candles are a gap down followed by two down candles, a bearish continuation (Bulkowski: 68%).

## Identical three crows, advance block, stalled pattern
tags: identical_three_crows advance_block stalled_pattern deliberation three crows tiring rally
Identical three crows are three long down candles each opening at the previous close: panic selling. The advance block is three up candles with shrinking bodies and growing upper wicks: the rally is tiring, not reversing yet. The stalled (deliberation) pattern is two strong up candles followed by a small one riding on the second's close: buyers are hesitating.

## Three stars in the south, unique three river, stick sandwich, concealing baby swallow
tags: three_stars_in_south unique_three_river stick_sandwich concealing_baby_swallow rare bottom
Rare bottoming shapes from Steve Nison's catalogue. Three stars in the south: three shrinking down candles with higher lows, the last tiny and wickless. Unique three river: a big down candle, a lower low that closes back inside it, then a small up candle below. Stick sandwich: two down candles closing at the same price with an up candle between them, a floor at that close. Concealing baby swallow: two full down candles, a gap down with a wick back up, then a down candle that swallows it. They are too rare to rely on alone.

## Tasuki gaps, gap three methods, side-by-side white lines
tags: tasuki_gap_up tasuki_gap_down gap_three_methods_up gap_three_methods_down side_by_side_white_up side_by_side_white_down gap continuation
Continuation patterns built around a gap in a trend. Tasuki: a candle against the trend dips into the gap but doesn't close it, so the trend continues (Bulkowski: the upside tasuki gap continued 57%). Gap three methods: a candle against the trend fills the gap completely, a quick reset before the trend resumes. Side-by-side white lines: two similar up candles after a gap, opening at the same price; above an up-gap the trend continues, below a down-gap the bounce is weak.

## Breakaway, ladder bottom, mat hold, rising and falling three methods
tags: breakaway_bull breakaway_bear ladder_bottom mat_hold rising_three_methods falling_three_methods five candle continuation
Five-candle patterns. Breakaway: a gap that runs out of steam over three candles, then a strong candle closing back into the gap (the climax is over). Ladder bottom: four falling down candles, the last with a long upper wick, then a gap up. Rising three methods: a strong up candle, three small candles drifting inside its range, then a strong close above it; falling three methods is the mirror. Mat hold is a stronger rising three methods whose pullback holds above a gap. They describe healthy pauses in trends.

## Hikkake and modified hikkake
tags: hikkake_bull hikkake_bear hikkake_mod_bull hikkake_mod_bear inside bar false breakout trap
Dan Chesler's hikkake: an inside candle, then a break one way (a "fake"), then within three candles a close back beyond the inside candle's other side. The breakout traders who were fooled have to get out, fuelling the move. The modified version needs two nested inside candles, the second closing near its extreme. It is the candlestick cousin of Wyckoff's spring and upthrust.

## Kicker (kicking)
tags: kicking_bull kicking_bear kicker marubozu gap reversal
A full down candle (no wicks) followed by a full up candle that gaps above it, or the mirror: a violent change of opinion, often on overnight news. Rare and among the strongest reversal signals; TA-Lib's "kicking by length" is the same shape judged by the longer candle.

## Harami cross, homing pigeon, matching low and high
tags: harami_cross_bull harami_cross_bear homing_pigeon matching_low matching_high harami doji
A harami cross is a doji inside the previous big candle: the move stopped dead. A homing pigeon is a small down candle inside a big down candle: selling shrinking. Matching low: two down candles closing at the same price, a possible floor (but Bulkowski found it continued down 61% of the time); matching high is the top version.

## Counterattack and separating lines
tags: counterattack_bull counterattack_bear separating_lines_bull separating_lines_bear meeting lines
Counterattack (meeting) lines: after a big candle one way, a big candle the other way opens far away but closes right at the previous close; the opposite side has fought back to level. Separating lines: in a trend, a candle against it followed by a candle opening at the same price and running with the trend; the trend reasserts itself.

## In-neck, on-neck and thrusting lines
tags: in_neck on_neck thrusting neck line bearish continuation weak bounce
After a big down candle the next opens below its low and bounces: closing at the low (on-neck), just inside the body (in-neck) or into the body but below its middle (thrusting). All three are weak bounces that usually fail: bearish continuation. Closing above the middle would make it a piercing line, which is bullish.

## Above and below the stomach
tags: above_the_stomach below_the_stomach stomach gap open midpoint
Above the stomach: after a down candle, an up candle that opens and closes above the down candle's middle: buyers took over overnight. Below the stomach is the bearish mirror. Bulkowski rates them among the more reliable two-candle reversals.

## Rising and falling windows (gaps)
tags: rising_window falling_window window gap up gap down japanese
"Window" is the Japanese name for a gap: the whole candle trades above the previous high (rising window) or below the previous low (falling window). Windows tend to act as support (rising) or resistance (falling) later; Japanese traders say "corrections go back to the window". A gap on big volume out of a range is a breakaway gap (see setups).

## Takuri, rickshaw man, high-wave candles
tags: takuri rickshaw_man high_wave doji long wick indecision
A takuri is a dragonfly doji whose lower wick is at least 80% of the range: a deep dip completely bought back, bullish after a fall. A rickshaw man is a long-legged doji with the open and close right in the middle of the range: perfect indecision. High-wave candles have small bodies with very long wicks both ways: the market has lost its sense of direction, often near turning points.

## Belt hold and closing marubozu
tags: belt_hold_bull belt_hold_bear closing_marubozu_bull closing_marubozu_bear marubozu opening closing
A bullish belt hold opens at its low and closes strongly higher (the Japanese "yorikiri"): buyers from the first trade, after a fall. The bearish belt hold opens at its high and falls all session. A closing marubozu closes at its extreme (no wick at the close): the winning side held control to the bell, which often carries into the next session.

## Long and short candles
tags: long_white_candle long_black_candle short_line long line short line body size
A long candle (body 1.5× the usual with short wicks) shows one side dominating; a run of them is a strong trend, a single one at the end of a long move can be a climax. A short candle (small body, small range) is a quiet, undecided session; several in a row are a coil before a move. On their own these are descriptions, not signals.
