# Schools of market analysis

## Dow theory
tags: dow theory charles dow hamilton primary trend confirmation averages
Charles Dow's ideas (1890s, organised by William Hamilton and Robert Rhea): the market discounts everything; there are three trends (primary over months to years, secondary corrections over weeks to months, minor daily noise); a primary trend has accumulation, public participation and distribution phases; the averages must confirm each other (industrials and transports); volume confirms the trend; a trend lasts until it gives definite reversal signals (a lower high and a lower low). Higher highs and higher lows, the basis of Kairos's swing structure, come from Dow.

## Elliott wave theory
tags: elliott wave ralph nelson elliott impulse corrective abc fibonacci waves
Ralph Nelson Elliott (1938) proposed that crowds move in five-wave impulses with the trend (1-2-3-4-5) and three-wave corrections against it (A-B-C), nested at every scale, with wave lengths often in Fibonacci ratios. Rules: wave 2 never retraces all of wave 1, wave 3 is never the shortest, wave 4 doesn't overlap wave 1 (in stocks). It is a useful way to think about trend maturity, but counts are subjective and can always be re-labelled afterwards; tests of its predictive power are weak. Kairos uses the parts that are measurable (swings, Fibonacci ratios, three drives) rather than wave counts.

## Wyckoff method
tags: wyckoff accumulation distribution spring upthrust composite man markup markdown
Richard Wyckoff (1930s): prices move in four phases driven by large operators (the "composite man"): accumulation (a range where the strong buy from the weak, ending with a spring and a sign of strength), markup (the uptrend), distribution (a range at the top, ending with an upthrust), and markdown. His three laws: supply and demand, cause and effect (the width of the range sets the size of the move), and effort versus result (volume should match price movement). Kairos detects springs and upthrusts and reads volume for effort and result.

## Volume spread analysis and effort versus result
tags: vsa volume spread analysis tom williams effort result no demand no supply climax
Tom Williams' volume spread analysis grew from Wyckoff: compare each candle's range and close with its volume. A wide up candle on huge volume closing off its high can be a buying climax; a narrow up candle on low volume shows "no demand". Big effort (volume) with little result (price) means the other side is absorbing it.

## Smart money concepts (SMC / ICT)
tags: smart money concepts smc ict order block fair value gap liquidity sweep break of structure change of character
A recent online school (popularised by Michael Huddleston, "ICT"): order blocks (the last opposite candle before a strong move, said to mark institutional orders), fair value gaps (a three-candle gap where the middle candle's range isn't overlapped), liquidity sweeps (runs on obvious highs or lows to trigger stops, like Wyckoff's spring), break of structure and change of character (Dow's swing structure renamed). Some ideas are old ones in new words; the claims about institutional intent are unproven. Kairos measures the mechanical parts (swings, false breaks, gaps).

## Market profile and volume profile
tags: market profile volume profile point of control value area auction market theory steidlmayer
J. Peter Steidlmayer's market profile (CBOT, 1980s) sees each session as an auction: the price with the most time or volume is the point of control, and the range holding about 70% of the volume is the value area. Prices accepted outside value start new trends; rejected ones return. Volume profile needs volume at each price (intraday data); Agora approximates important prices with support and resistance zones built from swings and volume.

## Japanese charting: Sakata's rules, Renko, Kagi, three-line break, point and figure
tags: japanese charting munehisa homma sakata renko kagi three line break point and figure
Munehisa Homma, an 18th-century rice trader in Sakata, is credited with the ideas behind candlesticks. Japan also gave Renko (bricks of fixed price size, ignoring time), Kagi (lines that change thickness on breakouts) and three-line break charts (a new line only when the price breaks the last three). Point and figure (from the US, 1880s) plots Xs and Os for rises and falls of a box size, filtering time out completely. All are ways to remove noise; Agora uses time-based candles, and Heikin-Ashi as its noise-filtered view.

## W.D. Gann
tags: gann angles square of nine time cycles
W.D. Gann (1878–1955) taught price-time angles (the 1×1 at 45°), the square of nine and time cycles. His methods are widely sold and hard to test; no robust evidence supports them. Kairos explains them on request but doesn't use them.

## Tom DeMark's indicators
tags: demark td sequential td setup countdown exhaustion
Tom DeMark's TD Sequential counts nine consecutive closes higher (or lower) than the close four bars earlier (a "setup"), then a thirteen-bar "countdown", to spot exhaustion. Popular with institutional desks; results vary by market. It isn't in Agora's catalog yet.

## Fibonacci tools
tags: fibonacci retracement extension fan arc time zones golden ratio 0.618
Leonardo of Pisa's sequence gives the ratios 0.382, 0.5 (not Fibonacci, but traded), 0.618, 0.786, 1.272 and 1.618. Traders use them for pullback levels (retracements), targets (extensions), sloped levels (fans), and time (time zones). They work best where they coincide with other support or resistance; on their own the evidence is thin, but so many traders watch them that reactions at 61.8% are common.

## Intermarket analysis
tags: intermarket analysis john murphy bonds stocks commodities dollar correlations
John Murphy's intermarket analysis links four markets: bonds usually turn before stocks, and stocks before commodities; a rising dollar weighs on commodities and emerging markets; rising oil hurts oil importers like India (rupee, inflation, current account) and helps producers. Gold rises in fear and with falling real interest rates. Watching these helps judge whether a news shock is local or global.

## Market breadth
tags: breadth advance decline line mcclellan percent above 200 day moving average new highs new lows
Breadth measures how many stocks join a move: the advance-decline line, the McClellan oscillator, the share of stocks above their 50- or 200-day averages, new highs minus new lows. Indices rising on narrowing breadth (a few giants carrying them) is a classic late-trend warning; a breadth thrust (90% of stocks rising in a few days) has often marked the start of new bull markets. Kairos's market overview reports breadth for the Nifty 50 and the US list.

## Sentiment: VIX, India VIX, put-call ratio, surveys
tags: sentiment vix india vix fear gauge put call ratio aaii contrarian
The VIX (S&P 500 options) and India VIX (Nifty options) measure expected volatility over the next 30 days: under 15 is calm, above 25–30 is fear, above 40 panic. Extreme fear has often come near lows ("be greedy when others are fearful"), but fear can grow for weeks first. Put-call ratios and investor surveys (AAII) are contrarian at extremes. Agora doesn't download VIX yet; Kairos reads fear from the news gauge and from price action.

## Seasonality: the documented calendar effects
tags: seasonality santa claus rally sell in may january effect diwali muhurat turn of month presidential cycle
Documented calendar effects: turn-of-the-month strength, the pre-holiday drift, "sell in May and go away" (weaker May–October returns in many markets), the Santa Claus rally (last five days of December and first two of January), the US presidential cycle (year three strongest), and India's Diwali Muhurat session (a one-hour ritual trading session, usually positive). Effects are small and unreliable after costs; never trade on a calendar alone.
